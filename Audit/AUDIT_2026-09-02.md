# Project Audit Report

## Audit Mode

`full` audit using the `web application` profile, with an emphasis on UI, responsive behavior, public portfolio credibility, release readiness, production parity, and read-only live smoke testing.

## Gate Decision

**GO for the local full-portfolio candidate. NO-GO for production publication until content-currentness and business workflow gates close. GO WITH WATCH ITEMS for continued operation of the current fail-closed public preview.**

The verified public release is stable and its security boundaries are behaving as designed. The local candidate repairs the two unreadable light-mode surfaces, corrects the theme-control state, removes the observed mobile overflow, improves the zero-jobs experience, and replaces internal implementation labels on key marketing surfaces. A second parity pass now integrates the legacy company profile, correctly labelled mission and vision, five core values, six service families, seven leadership records, full organizational chart, seven offices, and all 27 portfolio cards and assets. The public acquisition journeys still intentionally do not collect enquiries or applications, publish approved jobs, or expose an approved contact channel.

## Executive Summary

The live site was exercised in an actual browser across all 29 governed public routes, a branded 404, desktop and mobile layouts, the mobile menu, and both color themes. Every route rendered one main landmark and one H1, no public page produced a browser console warning/error, no rendered image was broken, and the representative HTTP matrix returned the expected 200/403/404 responses. After local remediation and content-parity integration, verification passes 12/12 static controls and 30/30 Playwright tests, including synthetic applicant, workforce, upload, staff, leadership, office, client-portfolio, and service-content workflows.

The strongest material UI issues were a theme-token mismatch, reversed theme-button semantics, and a 14 px mobile overflow in the in-app Chromium viewport. The local candidate closes all three and adds rendered regression coverage. It also gives the zero-jobs state an honest, useful presentation and removes internal pre-release language from the homepage, About, and Workforce surfaces without inventing claims.

The deeper portfolio constraint is intentional but material: job publication, applicant/workforce collection, resume upload, staff workflows, and indexing remain code-locked off; no approved public contact channel is published; and public legal/content pages still describe themselves as pre-release or approval-pending. Those gates correctly protect people and the business, but they prevent the site from functioning as a complete lead-generation, recruitment, and discoverability portfolio.
