# Project Audit Report

## Audit Mode

Codebase-quality and page-readiness audit with a focused, read-only production smoke test.

## Gate Decision

**NO-GO for presenting all 43 routes as a finished public portfolio.** The application is technically healthy and the core experience is usable, but the current information architecture publishes too many approval-dependent, empty-in-substance, or intentionally disabled destinations. **GO for the 15 strong core pages and conditional GO for three populated company-profile pages after owner verification.** Cleanup and consolidation are recommended before enabling public indexing or positioning the entire route set as complete.

## Executive Summary

The project contains 43 distinct page routes or route templates. None of the main public pages is literally blank, but several visually polished destinations are holding pages: they explain what may exist later rather than delivering the proof, articles, downloads, case studies, contact details, jobs, or active forms a visitor expects.

| Classification | Count | Decision |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Strong and useful now | 15 | Retain |
| Populated and useful, but owner/currentness verification remains | 3 | Retain after content verification |
| Useful only as an interim or gated explanation | 9 | Simplify, merge, or keep out of prominent navigation |
| Empty-in-substance future content hubs | 3 | Hide or retire until real content exists |
| Future/gated functional route templates | 13 | Keep in code, but do not present as generally available |
| **Total** | **43** | **Reduce the promoted public surface** |

The public header itself is controlled: it exposes six navigation labels plus the Access CTA. The larger burden appears in the footer, which exposes 16 non-home destinations, and in leaf pages that contain five to ten sections of governance-heavy copy. The visual system is cohesive, but some pages make the organization sound pre-release, uncertain, or internally focused.

## Audit Plan and Coverage

- **Profile:** PHP-backed public company website with applicant and recruitment portals.
- **Scope:** All 43 fixed or dynamic page templates; page purpose, content completeness, visitor usefulness, live availability, navigation exposure, overlap, and readiness language.
- **Live target:** `https://taascor.com/` and its anonymous public routes.
- **Known-good baseline:** Application commit `887a3f1789d7c9a0b95c1fd6e418831060f239a3`; documentation HEAD `d581643539fdd30db853ea65b35e78ddabc31659`.
- **Data sources:** Route/source inventory, `sitemap.php`, `router.php`, content and claim register, automated test route inventory, live HTML, HTTP behavior, rendered browser DOM, and representative desktop screenshots.
- **Roles/workflows:** Anonymous visitor, prospective employer, applicant entry, portal chooser, and unauthenticated staff entry.
- **Non-mutating limits:** No public form submission, production login, production data mutation, source remediation, commit, push, or deployment.
- **Exclusions:** Authenticated production applicant/staff records, final legal interpretation, physical-device testing, and content-owner approval decisions.

## Recommended Lean Public Architecture

1. **Primary navigation:** Solutions, Industries, Clients, About, Careers, plus the Access CTA.
2. **About family:** About, Leadership, Locations, and a concise verified Contact route.
3. **Services family:** Solutions hub plus the six service details; move Platform into HRIS-enabled Operations until a real product tour exists.
4. **Trust family:** Keep only approved Privacy, Terms, Accessibility, and Recruitment Safety links in the footer. Reintroduce Proof when it contains actual current evidence.
5. **Content family:** Hide Case Studies, Insights, and Resources until each contains at least one approved item.
6. **Functional family:** Keep applicant and staff routes unindexed and reachable only from a valid role/job/state. Do not advertise unavailable creation or collection actions.

This retains the breadth in the codebase while presenting a much calmer visitor experience. It avoids deleting future work and makes availability explicit at the correct entry point.

## Executive Decision View

The website is not technically broken and does not need 43 pages removed from the codebase. The issue is publication discipline: future functionality, internal governance explanations, draft legal content, and empty content hubs are too visible to ordinary visitors. Keep the strong service and company story, verify the populated corporate facts, and make unfinished experiences disappear from global navigation until they deliver real value.

The immediate decision is whether to approve the recommended lean architecture: remove Case Studies, Insights, and Resources from navigation; merge Platform and Proof into stronger existing pages; keep unavailable applicant/staff/workforce actions behind a clear availability boundary; and replace or hide unapproved legal drafts.

## Final Recommendation

Approve a local-only information-architecture remediation next. Preserve all future route code, but reduce the promoted public experience to the useful core, reconcile claim status, and make every visible CTA lead to an available outcome. Review the local result across desktop and mobile before separately approving commit, push, or deployment.

## Recurring / Systemic Risks

The project repeatedly distinguishes technically implemented pages from production-approved business capability, but the visitor-facing architecture does not consistently preserve that distinction. This is the same control category previously seen when local readiness and production completion were conflated. Prevent recurrence with a page-publication register containing `route`, `audience`, `content owner`, `business state`, `navigation state`, `indexing state`, `approval date`, `review date`, and `activation dependency`. The site owner should own the register; business owners should approve their rows before each public release.

## Local Remediation Addendum — 2026-09-04

The recommended lean architecture has now been implemented in the local working tree as a release candidate. No source page was deleted. Future and approval-gated routes remain available in the repository for later completion, but they are no longer promoted as finished public experiences.

### Changes Implemented

- Reduced the primary public navigation to Solutions, Industries, Clients, About, Careers, Contact, and the Access CTA.
- Removed Platform, Proof, Case Studies, Insights, Resources, draft Privacy, and draft Terms from global discovery surfaces.
- Reduced the XML sitemap from the broader route inventory to 19 current canonical public routes.
- Redirected promoted workforce, platform, and proof CTAs toward current Solutions, HRIS-enabled Operations, Clients, About, or Contact journeys.
- Guarded applicant registration and staff sign-in front doors using their existing production availability controls. Unavailable workflows now show an explicit status instead of inviting an action that cannot complete.
- Kept the applicant privacy link hidden unless applicant collection is enabled and the notice is no longer draft.
- Preserved light mode as the default, mobile motion, mobile tap-to-advance, and reduced-motion accessibility behavior.
- Hid the desktop progress readout at narrower desktop widths so it cannot collide with the expanded navigation.
